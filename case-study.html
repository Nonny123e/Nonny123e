Case study: UX strategy and web experience

Fabrics & Curtains:
a showroom built for the web.

A portfolio concept for a premium fabrics brand—bringing product discovery, interior inspiration and quote requests into one considered digital experience.

The challenge

Beautiful products need a better way to be discovered.

A fabric and curtains business needs more than a digital brochure. Visitors should be able to browse collections, understand available services, see how products look in real spaces and take the next step without confusion.

The experience should also be manageable as the catalogue grows, so product details, collections and project stories can be updated without redesigning the whole site.

UX strategy

From browsing to confidence.

01 / DISCOVER

Find the right fit

Organise browsing around customer needs such as material, colour, style, room and use case.

02 / UNDERSTAND

Make details clear

Give products context through descriptions, specifications, applications and related options.

03 / TRUST

Show the possibilities

Use project imagery, clear service explanations and process details to help people decide.

04 / ACT

Make the next step easy

Place quote and consultation calls to action where they feel natural, not intrusive.

Information architecture

A structure designed to grow with the business.

The navigation gives visitors clear routes into products, services and inspiration while keeping contact close at hand.

Technology stack

Three core technologies.
One connected experience.

HTML, CSS and JavaScript each have a clear role: structure, presentation and interaction. The goal is not an arbitrary percentage split, but a balanced use of the right tool for each job.

</>Structure

HTML

Builds the page structure with semantic sections, headings, links, forms and accessible content hierarchy.

The foundation: meaningful content and a clear document structure.

#stylePresentation

CSS

Defines the visual system—typography, spacing, colour, responsive grids, layouts, hover states and motion.

The visual layer: responsive, consistent and recognisably on-brand.

{ JS }Interaction

JavaScript

Adds behaviour where needed, such as mobile navigation, filtering, form feedback and small interactive details.

The behaviour layer: useful interactions without unnecessary complexity.
